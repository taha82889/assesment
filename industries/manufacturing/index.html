AI and automation for Manufacturing

Smart Logics applies computer vision and automation on the factory floor: visual quality inspection, safety monitoring, predictive maintenance signals and paperwork automation. These systems help find defects earlier, reduce stoppages and give supervisors time back, provided cameras, lighting and data are set up properly.

What can Smart Logics offer manufacturers, plant managers and industrial operations teams?

Visual quality inspection

Cameras and models that spot defects, missing parts and labelling errors on the line, with images saved for review.

Safety and compliance monitoring

Detection of people in restricted zones, missing PPE and unsafe forklift movement, with alerts to supervisors.

Predictive maintenance signals

Models on sensor and maintenance data that flag equipment likely to need attention.

Production reporting automation

Shift reports, downtime logs and quality records generated from machine and operator data.

How does automation increase revenue?

  • Fewer rejected shipments and returns protect margin and customer trust.
  • More output from existing lines through less unplanned downtime.
  • Faster response to quote and delivery requests.

How much working time can it save?

  • Inspectors focus on exceptions.
  • Supervisors spend less time on reports.
  • Maintenance teams plan work instead of reacting.

Hours saved are roughly tasks per month × minutes per task × share of tasks automated. We fill that in with your own numbers during discovery, so the estimate comes from your operation and not from a generic claim.

Where does the efficiency gain come from?

  • Defects found earlier in the process.
  • Fewer unplanned stops.
  • Consistent records for audits.

How do we measure success?

We agree a baseline before building, then track:

  • Defect escape rate and scrap rate
  • Unplanned downtime hours
  • Overall equipment effectiveness (OEE)
  • Hours spent on manual reporting

How does a project with Smart Logics work?

  1. Discovery. We map your workflow, data and systems, and agree what success means.
  2. Architecture and pilot plan. We choose the smallest useful first release and how it connects to your tools.
  3. Sprint delivery. Working software every sprint, tested on your real data.
  4. Release and iteration. Monitoring, quality checks and improvements after launch.

Inspection accuracy must be proven on your own parts and lighting. We validate on your samples before any production rollout.

Common questions

Do we need new cameras or sensors?

Sometimes. We start with a site visit or sample footage to check lighting, angles and speeds, then say whether existing equipment is enough.

Can models run on the factory floor without internet?

Yes. Edge deployment is a common approach for vision systems, with a plan for monitoring and updates.

How long until an inspection pilot is running?

It depends on data access and line access. We scope a pilot after discovery rather than promising a timeline in advance.
